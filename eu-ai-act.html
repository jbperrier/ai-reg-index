EU Artificial Intelligence Act

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 of the European Parliament and of the Council
of 13 June 2024, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744
OJ L, 2024/1689, 12.7.2024

The first horizontal statute to regulate artificial intelligence as a class. It sorts systems into risk tiers and attaches duties to each tier, binding any provider or deployer whose output reaches the Union.

Scope extraterritorial

Who it binds

Providers who place an AI system on the Union market and deployers who use one under their own authority. Establishment in the Union is not required. A provider in Oregon whose model output reaches a user in Germany is inside the scope.

Military, national security and pure research uses fall outside it.

Art. 5 2 Feb 2025
in force

Prohibited practices

Social scoring by public authorities, untargeted scraping of facial images to build recognition databases, emotion inference in workplaces and schools, and systems that exploit age or disability to distort behaviour. These are banned outright, with no conformity route.

Art. 53, 55 2 Aug 2025
in force

General‑purpose models

Technical documentation, a public summary of training data, and a policy for complying with Union copyright law. Models judged to carry systemic risk add adversarial evaluation, incident reporting and cybersecurity obligations on top.

Art. 50 2 Aug 2026
pending

Transparency

A person must be told when they are dealing with an AI system. Synthetic image, audio, video and text must be marked in a machine‑readable format. Deepfakes must be labelled as such.

Systems already on the market before this date have until 2 Dec 2026 for the marking requirement alone. The chatbot disclosure has no grace period.

Annex III 2 Aug 2026
2 Dec 2027
deferred

High‑risk systems

Systems used in employment, credit, education, essential services, law enforcement and biometrics. The duties are heavy: a risk management system, data governance, technical documentation, automatic logging, human oversight, and a conformity assessment before the system reaches the market.

The Digital Omnibus moved this tier back by roughly sixteen months. It is the single largest change to the Act since it was published.

Art. 99 penalties

What non‑compliance costs

€35M / 7% Prohibited practices under Article 5
€15M / 3% Most other obligations, transparency included
€7.5M / 1% Supplying incorrect information to an authority

Percentages are of worldwide annual turnover; the higher of the two applies. Authorities may also order a model withdrawn from the Union market.
